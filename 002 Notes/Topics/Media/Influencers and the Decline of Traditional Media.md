## Working argument

Social media has shifted credibility from institutions toward recognizable individuals. Audiences increasingly
choose information and entertainment through a perceived relationship with the sender. Trust signals now come
from personality, consistency, design, and communication style as much as from institutional reputation.

Donald Trump's attention-driven political style is one example of this shift. The same pattern appears in
media consumption: a familiar creator can feel more relevant than a publishing brand.

## Pressure on traditional media

Traditional media once offered scarce access to primary sources and professional interpretation. Access is now
broadly available, weakening the gatekeeping function. Publishers must compete on analysis, context, and user
experience.

Even respected outlets can lose habitual readers through small but repeated failures: poor app interactions,
awkwardly produced interviews, or content that feels detached from its audience. Individual creators often
provide more engaging analysis for a narrower community.

## Open question

How can a publisher build durable personal trust around journalists and formats while retaining the
verification, editing, and institutional accountability that distinguish professional media?
