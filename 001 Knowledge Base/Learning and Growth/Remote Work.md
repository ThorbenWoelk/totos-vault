## Research summary

Research on remote work reports mixed effects on productivity and well-being.

## Productivity study

*Work from Home and Productivity: Evidence from Personnel and Analytics Data on Information Technology
Professionals* studied 10,000 skilled employees at an Indian technology company.

The source note records these findings:

- Hours worked increased.
- Output declined slightly.
- Productivity fell by 8–19%.
- Coordination and meeting time increased.
- Uninterrupted work time decreased.
- Employees interacted with fewer people and business units.
- One-to-one meetings with supervisors became less frequent.

## Possible drivers

1. **Meeting load:** Time spent in mostly virtual meetings remained high after offices reopened.
2. **Interrupted work:** A “call-center atmosphere” reduced sustained focus time.
3. **Reduced informal networking:** Productive, unplanned interaction became less common.

## Well-being

Potential risks include telework-related stress and video-call fatigue.

Kornadt et al. (2025), published in *BMC Public Health*, followed 3,403 employed participants across five
European countries. After controlling for relevant covariates, working from home was negatively associated
with well-being early in the pandemic and was unrelated to well-being later on.
