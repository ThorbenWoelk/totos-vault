A system for personal development should be designed around what research knows about human behavior change, learning, and wellbeing. The following principles inform its structure:

1. Active retrieval > recognition
2. If-then rules > mere intention
3. Intrinsic motivation > extrinsic motivation
4. Social connection is key
5. Rumination != development

### On retrieval and learning

**Active retrieval** - being forced to generate an answer from memory rather than recognize it - produces substantially stronger retention and more durable learning.

- Roediger, H. L., & Karpicke, J. D. (2006). The power of testing memory. *Psychological Science*, 17(3), 249-255.
- Dunlosky, J., et al. (2013). Improving students' learning with effective learning techniques. *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*, 14(1), 4-58.

### On implementation intentions

Vague intentions ("I want to apply for more jobs") rarely translate into action. Specific if-then plans ("On Monday and Thursday mornings, I will write one application before opening anything else") dramatically increase follow-through. Each active goal specifies not just what, but when, where, and how. Helps a sense of commitment instead of wishful thinking.

- Gollwitzer, P. M. (1999). Implementation intentions: Strong effects of simple plans. *American Psychologist*, 54(7), 493-503.

### On intrinsic motivation

Goals pursued for intrinsic reasons - genuine interest, alignment with personal values, sense of competence and autonomy - show greater persistence and better outcomes than goals driven by external pressure or obligation. Self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan) distinguishes three basic psychological needs: **autonomy, competence, and relatedness**. Goals that serve all three tend to stick. Goals that violate any tend to erode.

### On social connection

Inadequate social connection increases mortality risk by approximately 26-29%, comparable in magnitude to smoking 15 cigarettes a day.

- Holt-Lunstad, J., Smith, T. B., Baker, M., Harris, T., & Stephenson, D. (2015). Loneliness and social isolation as risk factors for mortality. *Perspectives on Psychological Science*, 10(2), 227-237.

### On the default mode network and attention

The brain's Default Mode Network (DMN) - active during self-referential thought, mind-wandering, and rumination - competes directly with the task-positive network needed for focused work. In individuals with ADHD-like attention profiles, this competition is amplified: the DMN remains active during tasks that should suppress it, creating consistent interference with encoding, recall, and sustained focus.

- Buckner, R. L., [[Jessica Andrews-Hanna|Andrews-Hanna]], J. R., & Schacter, D. L. (2008). The brain's default network. *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences*, 1124, 1-38.

### On insight vs. behavior change

Metacognitive therapy research (Wells, 2009) demonstrates that understanding the mechanism of a thought pattern is insufficient to change it. What changes behavior is behavioral experiment - acting differently and observing the result - combined with reflection. Insight is the prerequisite, not the solution.

### On flow

Csikszentmihalyi's research on optimal experience identifies conditions that reliably **produce flow: clear goals, immediate feedback, challenge matched to skill, and removal of distraction.** 

- Csikszentmihalyi, M. (1990). *Flow: The Psychology of Optimal Experience*. Harper & Row.
